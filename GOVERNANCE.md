# Governance

Aristo is an open-source project maintained by Aretta. This document
says who decides what — and how that changes as the project grows.

## The open-source commitment

The Aristo SDK — the CLI, the macros, `aristo-core`, the index
format, the skills — is MIT-licensed, and stays that way. Aretta runs
a paid backend (deeper machine-checking, hosted solutions) for teams
that need it, but that's a service *around* the SDK, not a gate
*inside* it: the open-source experience doesn't degrade because you
don't pay.

## Who decides

Aristo is early, and small. Today the maintainers — the founding team
at Aretta — make the calls:

- **Routine changes** (fixes, docs, incremental features) land by pull
  request, reviewed by a maintainer.
- **Load-bearing decisions** (the annotation model, the verify
  spectrum, anything that changes the contract with users) are
  discussed in the open first — an issue or a Discussion — before
  they're built, and we write down the *why*.

We decide in public and record the reasoning, so the project's
direction stays legible even while the team is small.

## Maintainers

Right now the maintainers are Aretta's founders. As contributors show
up and stick around — consistent, high-quality work and good judgment
in review — we'll invite them in. Maintainership is earned through
contribution, not appointed.

## How this evolves

This is stage-one governance: founder-led, deliberately lightweight —
the right shape for an alpha project with a handful of people. As the
community grows — more maintainers, more external contributors, higher
stakes — we'll formalize: a written decision process, a maintainer
team with explicit roles, and a clear path for resolving
disagreements. We'll make those changes in the open, here, when the
project needs them — not before.

## Aretta's role

Aretta funds and maintains Aristo and builds the paid backend it
connects to. The company backs the project, but the open-source SDK
stands on its own: MIT-licensed, usable offline, not contingent on any
Aretta service. If the two ever pull in different directions, this
document — and the license — are the commitment that holds.
